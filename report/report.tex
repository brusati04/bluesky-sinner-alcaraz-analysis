\begin{abstract}
This study presents a comprehensive, end-to-end social network analysis (SNA) and natural language processing (NLP) evaluation of the digital footprint surrounding the Jannik Sinner versus Carlos Alcaraz tennis rivalry on the decentralized microblogging platform Bluesky. By crawling \textbf{7,339 unique posts} strictly bounded within the temporal window of the \textbf{US Open 2025 (August 25 to September 8, 2025)}, we examine the structural and semantic properties of social communication. Graph topology is modeled under two paradigms: an Undirected Graph representing mutual conversational co-attention, and a Directed Graph representing explicit conversational flows. 

Through Louvain community detection, we identify a highly modular structure ($Q = 0.9678$, 181 communities). Natural language processing models—including VADER sentiment scoring, NRC emotion lexicon matching, and DBpedia Spotlight Named Entity Linking (NEL)—reveal a public discourse dominated by anticipatory tension and fear-related clusters, reflecting match outcomes and Sinner's doping controversy. Finally, by mapping community partitions to sentiment score distributions, a Kruskal-Wallis H-test ($H = 116.6158$, $p = 0.0000$) reveals statistically significant attitudinal polarization, demonstrating that structurally isolated sub-communities carry highly distinct emotional and attitudinal registers.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The rivalry between Jannik Sinner and Carlos Alcaraz has emerged as the defining narrative of modern men's tennis, marking a generational transition at the helm of the ATP Tour. This rivalry is not only played out on the court but also leaves a rich digital footprint across social media platforms. 

This paper investigates the conversational structures and sentiments surrounding these two players on the decentralized social network \textbf{Bluesky} during the \textbf{US Open 2025}. Specifically, this study addresses three core Research Questions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{RQ1: Structural Characteristics.} What are the structural differences between modeled undirected (co-attention) and directed (conversational flows) interaction networks? Does a Giant Connected Component emerge?
    \item \textbf{RQ2: Attitudinal Polarization.} Do isolated conversational sub-communities exhibit statistically significant differences in their sentiments?
    \item \textbf{RQ3: Semantic Profile.} What are the dominant emotional registers and entities linked in the public discourse surrounding Sinner and Alcaraz?
\end{enumerate}

By building a consolidated pipeline that executes undirected and directed social network analysis alongside advanced natural language processing, this study provides a unified empirical look at microblogging tennis dynamics.

\section{Data Collection \& Preprocessing}
Data was crawled directly from the Bluesky platform utilizing a custom script executing day-by-day queries to bypass the API's standard pagination limit. The collection query targeted keywords associated with the players (e.g. \texttt{sinner}, \texttt{alcaraz}, \texttt{jannik}, \texttt{carlitos}) and was strictly bounded between \textbf{August 25, 2025} and \textbf{September 8, 2025}, matching the exact duration of the US Open tournament.

A total of \textbf{7,339 unique posts} were successfully retrieved. Preprocessing steps included:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Parsing list-based fields (mentions, hashtags, external links).
    \item Mapping author and target decentralized identifiers (DIDs) to human-readable handles (e.g., \texttt{thetennisletter.bsky.social}).
    \item Text normalization, removing URLs and handle tags for NLP processing while preserving emojis.
\end{itemize}

\section{Social Network Analysis (SNA)}
The social network was modeled under two structural paradigms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Undirected Network ($G$)}: Edges represent mutual co-attention where users are linked if one mentions or replies to another, collapsing directional flow.
    \item \textbf{Directed Network ($G_d$)}: Edges represent explicit conversational flow pointing from the initiator to the target ($User_A \rightarrow User_B$ if $User_A$ replies to or mentions $User_B$).
\end{enumerate}

The network displays a highly modular, fragmented topology. With $Q = 0.9678$, Louvain partitioning detects 181 communities, showing that conversations on Bluesky are organized in small, isolated threads rather than a single unified town square. The emergence of a Giant Connected Component (GCC) of 77 nodes represents 14.42\% of the network. The mean degree ($\langle k \rangle = 1.352$) is above the critical phase-transition threshold ($\langle k \rangle \sim 1.0$) from Random Graph theory, showing the early emergence of a core connected backbone.

Degree assortativity is weakly negative for both the undirected ($r = -0.0408$) and directed ($r = -0.0060$) network layouts, indicating disassortative mixing. This pattern is characteristic of hub-and-spoke star configurations, where highly active broadcaster and news accounts (e.g., \texttt{thetennisletter.bsky.social}) are linked to and by low-degree fan accounts. Conversely, community assortativity is exceptionally high ($0.9887$), demonstrating a very strong tendency for accounts to link to others within the same detected Louvain partitions. This confirms that despite the overall disassortative degree configuration, conversational boundaries are strictly defined within highly isolated local communities.

\section{Attitudinal Polarization Analysis}
To address \textbf{RQ2}, we map the structural communities identified by Louvain partitioning back to their post-level sentiment profiles. 

Only communities with at least 5 posts were considered to filter out isolated micro-threads (resulting in 47 valid communities). A non-parametric Kruskal-Wallis H-test was performed to evaluate if sentiment scores differ significantly across these communities.

The test yields:
\begin{equation*}
    H = 116.6158, \quad p = 0.0000 \quad (p < 0.001)
\end{equation*}

This extremely low $p$-value allows us to reject the null hypothesis of equal median sentiments. This provides empirical evidence of \textbf{attitudinal polarization}: structurally isolated fan groups do not merely discuss different players; they express statistically distinct attitudes. As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:polarization}, Community \#40 (dominated by sports journalism and analytical updates) and Community \#26 (dominated by fan celebrations and player support) show highly positive average sentiments, while other communities show neutral or negative sentiment offsets.

\begin{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
This study evaluated the Jannik Sinner and Carlos Alcaraz rivalry on Bluesky during the US Open 2025. 

Using a consolidated SNA and NLP pipeline on 7,339 posts, we proved that the conversational network is highly modular ($Q = 0.9678$, 181 communities) and exhibits significant attitudinal polarization ($p = 0.0000$), confirming that tennis fans cluster in isolated ideological echo chambers. Furthermore, NLP modeling identified anticipation and fear as the dominant emotional registers. 

Future work will expand the temporal window to evaluate long-term shifts in rivalry dynamics and incorporate deep-learning transformer models for semantic sarcasm detection.
