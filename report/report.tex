\begin{abstract}
This report explores how the tennis rivalry between \textbf{Jannik Sinner} and \textbf{Carlos Alcaraz} shaped the online discourse on the \textbf{Bluesky} social platform during the \textbf{2025 US Open}. Gathering a dataset of \textbf{9{,}078 posts} via a day-by-day crawling strategy, we reconstruct the network of replies and mentions to map how communities formed, while tracing the emotional and sentiment dynamics triggered by match outcomes. The analysis reveals a highly modular network where fans cluster around shared news hubs rather than forming isolated echo chambers. Furthermore, by integrating neural sentiment signals with user interaction frequencies, we demonstrate how a sentiment-aware stance classifier can successfully refine and correct the classification errors inherent in simpler, frequency-only baselines.

\end{abstract}

\section{Objective}

The goal of this project is to \textbf{characterise the online discourse} surrounding two of
tennis's biggest rivals---Jannik Sinner and Carlos Alcaraz---on the Bluesky social platform
during the 2025 US Open. Concretely the analysis aims to:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Describe} the social-network structure built from replies and mentions,
    identify cohesive communities, and measure user influence via centrality metrics.
    \item \textbf{Quantify} sentiment and emotion expressed in posts using state-of-the-art
    NLP models (RoBERTa, NRC lexicon, GoEmotions) and compare two emotion backends.
    \item \textbf{Assign a stance} (pro-Sinner, pro-Alcaraz or neutral) to each user by fusing
    sentiment signals with mention frequency, and evaluate how this refined stance differs from a
    simpler frequency-only baseline.
    \item \textbf{Visualise} all results to support interpretability---network graphs, sentiment
    time series, emotion profiles and stance distributions.
\end{itemize}

The phenomenon under study is the \textbf{evolution of fan engagement} during a high-stakes
Grand Slam, exploring how rivalry narratives translate into measurable patterns of sentiment,
community formation and opinion polarisation.

\section{Data}

\subsection{Collection strategy}

Data were collected from Bluesky using its official \texttt{atproto} API
(\texttt{src/crawler.py}). Because the search endpoint does not guarantee exhaustive retrieval
over a large window, a \textbf{day-by-day} crawling strategy was used:

\begin{itemize}
    \item For each 24-hour window from \texttt{2025-08-21} to \texttt{2025-09-10} (UTC), two
    keyword queries were issued---one \textbf{Sinner-oriented} and one
    \textbf{Alcaraz-oriented}---each capped at \textbf{5{,}000 posts/day} to respect rate limits
    while capturing peak tournament activity.
    \item A polite sleep was inserted between requests, and automatic retries with exponential
    backoff handled HTTP 429 rate-limiting.
    \item Results from both queries were merged and \textbf{deduplicated by post URI}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Preprocessing \& enrichment}

The raw CSV is transformed into a \textbf{frozen} enriched dataset
(\texttt{data/sinner\_alcaraz\_processed.csv}, 44 columns) that serves as the single source of
truth for all downstream stages. The enrichment (\texttt{src/preprocessing.py}) includes:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Text cleaning} (HTML unescape, URL/handle removal, normalisation) and
    \textbf{lemmatisation} (spaCy + TweetTokenizer).
    \item \textbf{RoBERTa sentiment} via
    \texttt{cardiffnlp/twitter-roberta-base-sentiment-latest}, producing a
    \texttt{sentiment\_category} (positive/neutral/negative) and a continuous
    \texttt{sentiment\_compound} $= P(\text{pos}) - P(\text{neg})$.
    \item \textbf{Emotion} with two backends: the \textbf{NRC lexicon} (NRCLex) over the 8
    Plutchik emotions, and \textbf{GoEmotions} (\texttt{SamLowe/roberta-base-go\_emotions}),
    whose 28 fine-grained labels are collapsed onto the same 8 categories for comparison.
    \item \textbf{Named entity recognition} (spaCy) and \textbf{entity linking} to detect
    mentions of Sinner or Alcaraz.
    \item \textbf{Post- and user-level stance} (see Section~\ref{sec:stancemethod}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Methodology}

The analytical pipeline is a strict DAG (orchestrated by \texttt{main.py}).

\subsection{Network construction}

Nodes are users; edges are reply/mention interactions. Two graphs are built: an
\textbf{undirected} graph $G_u$ (edge weight = interaction count) and a \textbf{directed} graph
$G_d$ (author $\rightarrow$ replied/mentioned user). For both we compute degree, closeness and
betweenness centrality, in-/out-degree and PageRank. Communities are detected with
\textbf{Louvain} on $G_u$ and \textbf{Infomap} on $G_d$. \textbf{Filtered subgraphs} (connected
components $\geq 10$ nodes, top communities by post volume) are used for visualisation and stance
analysis.

\subsection{Stance detection}
\label{sec:stancemethod}

Post-level stance assigns the RoBERTa compound score to the mentioned player for single-mention
posts; dual-mention posts are split via sentence-level sentiment. User-level net stance fuses
mean sentiment with mention frequency:

\[
\text{net\_stance} = 0.50\,(\text{mean}_\text{sinner} - \text{mean}_\text{alcaraz})
                   + 0.15\,(\text{freq}_\text{sinner} - \text{freq}_\text{alcaraz})
\]

(the remaining $0.35$ weight is reserved for a future emotion-based component). Users are
labelled \texttt{sinner} / \texttt{alcaraz} / \texttt{neutral} with a $\pm 0.05$ threshold.
Polarisation is measured via stance assortativity, the cross-stance edge ratio, and the standard
deviation of net stance.

\subsection{Stance \& polarisation}

The four-panel stance summary (Figure~\ref{fig:stancesummary}) gives the headline picture. Of
\textbf{2{,}296 analysed users}, most are \textbf{neutral (1{,}080)}, followed by
\textbf{Alcaraz (818)} and \textbf{Sinner (398)}---a tilt towards Alcaraz consistent with his
final victory. Net stance scores are tightly concentrated around zero (score SD $= 0.227$), and
the polarisation diagnostics are mild: stance assortativity is slightly \emph{negative}
($-0.047$) and the cross-stance edge ratio is $0.108$. In other words, despite the high
\emph{structural} modularity of the graph, users are \emph{not} strongly segregated by stance---
fans of opposing players still interact (often via shared news hubs), so this is not a classic
echo chamber.

\paragraph{Sentiment-aware vs.\ frequency baseline.}
Figure~\ref{fig:fanbasecmp} contrasts a frequency-only baseline with the refined,
sentiment-aware leaning. Folding RoBERTa sentiment into the stance moves users between classes:
the Sinner camp shrinks from \textbf{454 to 398}, Alcaraz edges up from \textbf{814 to 818}, and
neutral grows from \textbf{1{,}028 to 1{,}080}. The reclassified users are typically those who
\emph{mention} a player frequently but \emph{express} sentiment inconsistent with that mention
pattern (e.g.\ criticising the player they name most)---exactly the cases a raw mention count
mislabels. This demonstrates the value of fusing sentiment with frequency rather than counting
mentions alone.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Interpretation}

The analysis paints a picture of a \textbf{vibrant, on-topic, but only mildly polarised} online
community:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Structurally fragmented, not opinion-segregated.} The graph is extremely modular
    (Louvain $Q\approx0.97$) yet stance assortativity is near zero. Clustering is driven by
    \emph{who replies to whom} (frequently shared news/aggregator hubs) more than by partisan
    allegiance.
    \item \textbf{Positive, low-toxicity tone.} Most posts are neutral; among sentiment-bearing
    posts, positive outweighs negative $\approx 2.5{:}1$, and negative emotions (anger, disgust)
    are rare under both emotion backends.
    \item \textbf{Sentiment tracks the tournament.} The time series reacts to rounds and diverges
    by fanbase around the final, illustrating how match outcomes drive emotional response.
    \item \textbf{Sentiment-aware stance matters.} Refining a frequency baseline with sentiment
    reclassifies a meaningful slice of users, correcting cases where mention frequency and
    expressed sentiment disagree.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Strengths}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Reproducible DAG} with a frozen intermediate dataset, so any downstream stage
    can be re-run without recomputing the expensive NLP enrichment (seeds fixed at 42).
    \item \textbf{Two emotion backends} (lexicon vs.\ neural) provide robustness and expose how
    sensitive emotion attribution is to the scoring method.
    \item \textbf{Fine-grained stance} via sentence-level splitting of dual-mention posts.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Limitations \& future work}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Recall.} The Bluesky search API does not guarantee exhaustive retrieval; posts
    without the chosen keywords are missed.
    \item \textbf{Sarcasm \& irony.} RoBERTa sentiment can misread sarcastic praise/criticism;
    sarcasm detection is a natural extension.
    \item \textbf{Weight calibration.} The stance weights ($0.50$, $0.15$, with $0.35$ reserved)
    are heuristic; a data-driven fit against labelled stances would improve accuracy and let the
    reserved emotion component be activated.
    \item \textbf{Static communities.} Community detection is computed once over the whole window;
    a temporal (sliding-window) analysis could reveal how clusters form and dissolve around
    matches.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Conclusion}

By integrating social-network analysis, NLP-based sentiment/emotion mining and sentiment-aware
stance detection, this project characterises the Bluesky discourse around the Sinner--Alcaraz
rivalry at the 2025 US Open. The community is sparse and highly modular yet not opinion-segregated,
its tone is neutral-to-positive, its sentiment tracks match outcomes, and a sentiment-aware
stance classifier improves on a naive frequency baseline. The pipeline is modular and
reproducible, and readily adapts to other rivalries or platforms.
